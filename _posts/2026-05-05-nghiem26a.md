title: Balancing Safety and Helpfulness in Healthcare AI Assistants through Iterative
  Preference Alignment
abstract: Large Language Models ({LLM}s) are increasingly used in healthcare, yet
  ensuring their safety and trustworthiness remains a barrier to deployment. Conversational
  medical assistants must avoid unsafe compliance without over-refusing benign queries.
  We present an iterative post-deployment alignment framework that applies Kahneman–Tversky
  Optimization ({KTO}) and Direct Preference Optimization ({DPO}) to refine models
  against domain-specific safety signals. Using the {CARES}-18K benchmark for adversarial
  robustness, we evaluate four {LLM}s (Llama-3B/8B, Meditron-8B, Mistral-7B) across
  multiple cycles. Our results show up to 42% improvement in safety-related metrics
  for harmful query detection, alongside interesting trade-offs against erroneous
  refusals, thereby exposing architecture-dependent calibration biases. We also perform
  ablation studies to identify when self-evaluation is reliable and when external
  or finetuned judges are necessary to maximize performance gains. Our findings underscore
  the importance of adopting best practices that balance patient safety, user trust,
  and clinical utility in the design of conversational medical assistants.
